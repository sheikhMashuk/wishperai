You know the answer. The pressure just took the words.

A live interview measures a very specific thing: whether you can retrieve what you know while a stranger watches a timer. Most of us are worse at that than at the actual job.

WhisperAI is a floor, not a script. It gives you the first clean sentence and three points worth making, fast enough to use, quiet enough that the call never knows it's there. What you do with that is still on you.

Hears the room

It catches the question before you've stopped nodding.

Live transcription runs on the call audio. When a question lands and the interviewer pauses, WhisperAI answers on its own — no button, no hotword. Or type your own and hit Enter.

Seven recognition languages, and a WASAPI loopback tap so it hears their side without a virtual cable.

Not in the frame

The interviewer's screen recording has a WhisperAI-shaped hole in it.

It's a native window that asks the compositor to skip it whenever a frame is captured — WDA_EXCLUDEFROMCAPTURE on Windows, the private-window flag on macOS. Zoom, Meet, Teams, OBS, Game Bar: none of them see it.

No bot joins the call, so there's no participant and no "recording" banner either.

Your model, always current

No hard-coded model names to go stale.

Pick Groq, OpenAI, Anthropic, OpenRouter, or a local Ollama. WhisperAI reads that provider's live model list, picks a fast default, and if a provider retires a model mid-call it re-fetches and swaps one in without you noticing. More on that →

Groq

LPU inference, generous free tier. The right default for something you're reading in real time.

OpenRouter

One key, ~400 models including free ones. The dropdown fills itself from their API.

Local · Ollama

Point it at localhost:11434 and nothing leaves the machine. Slower, fully private.

What it won't do

It won't pass an interview you're not close to passing. The answers are a strong starting point, not a performance. If you can't carry the follow-up, the follow-up will show.

It's not magic on bad audio. Transcription is only as good as what reaches your speakers — muffled calls, heavy accents and crosstalk all degrade it.

It's a real desktop app. Rust build, a keychain entry, global hotkeys. Nothing shady, but it's software you install, not a browser tab.
