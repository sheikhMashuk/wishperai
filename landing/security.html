Stealth & privacy

Absent from the recording. Honest about the rest.

Two separate claims live on this page. One: the interviewer's screen capture doesn't contain the overlay. Two: nothing you say or ask is stored anywhere. Both are true, and both have edges worth knowing.

How the capture exclusion works

On Windows, every window can set a display-affinity flag that the Desktop Window Manager reads when it composes a capture frame. WhisperAI sets SetWindowDisplayAffinity(hwnd, WDA_EXCLUDEFROMCAPTURE) the moment its window is created. From then on the compositor renders that window to your monitor but omits it from any buffer handed to a capture API.

That's the same mechanism a lot of DRM video players and password managers use. It isn't a trick or an overlay hack — it's the OS honouring a request. macOS has an equivalent window-level flag, and the app sets it on that platform too.

What that covers

Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams and Webex screen share — whether they share the whole desktop or a single window. OBS Studio and other recorders built on the same capture APIs. The Windows Game Bar and Snipping Tool. Most remote-desktop software.

What it can't cover

A second camera or a phone pointed at your screen — that's just a photo of a monitor. A managed work laptop with a monitoring agent that captures at a lower level than DWM. A hardware capture card between your GPU and a second display. If any of those are in play, assume nothing is hidden.

No bot joins the call

Note-taker tools like Otter and Fireflies work by adding a participant to the meeting. That participant shows in the roster, often triggers a "this meeting is being recorded" notice, and is logged by the host's admin console.

WhisperAI adds nothing to the meeting. It reads audio locally, off your own sound output. The call software has no way to know it exists.

Where your data goes

The short version: from your machine to the model provider you chose, and nowhere else. There is no WhisperAI account, no WhisperAI server, and no analytics.

API key
Windows Credential Manager / macOS Keychain, via the system keyring API. Never a plaintext file.
settings + history
Provider, model, résumé text, language and the last ~40 answers — localStorage on that machine only. Cleared by the Clear button.
call audio
When you press Listen, short chunks of the call's system audio (not your mic) are sent to your transcription provider — Groq or OpenAI Whisper, using the same key. Nothing is written to disk.
prompts & answers
Sent directly to your provider's endpoint (or your local Ollama). Their retention policy applies, not ours — we don't have one because we never see them.
telemetry
None. No crash reporting, no usage pings, no update check that phones home with an id.

The client is source-available on GitHub. The network calls it makes are exactly the ones listed above; you can read them.

A word on using it

WhisperAI is built for the case where you know the material and the format is the problem — nerves, blank moments, a phrasing you can't reach under a timer.

Using it to fake competence you don't have is a bad trade. You'd be optimizing for the offer and against the job, and interviews that a tool gets you through tend to become jobs a tool can't.

Take-homes and proctored assessments that explicitly forbid assistance are a clear line. Don't.
