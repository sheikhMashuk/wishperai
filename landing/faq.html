WASAPI loopback capture on Windows — it reads whatever is playing out of your speakers or headphones, which is the other people on the call. Your own microphone is never opened. No virtual audio cable, no meeting bot; the call software isn't involved and can't see it. The audio is transcribed in short chunks by your provider's Whisper (Groq or OpenAI).

Groq, OpenAI, Anthropic, OpenRouter, or a local model through Ollama. WhisperAI fetches each provider's live model list and picks a fast default — so new models show up on their own and retired ones get swapped out without an error. Groq is the best starting point for speed.

The app is free and source-available. You pay whatever your model provider charges — which, on Groq's or OpenRouter's free tier, is nothing for interview-length usage. With local Ollama it's just your electricity.

No. There's no account and no WhisperAI server. Your key is in the OS keychain, settings are in local storage, the transcript is held in memory and dropped. Prompts go straight to your provider — their retention policy applies, not ours, because we never see them. Details here.

Windows 10 and 11 (64-bit) is the primary build and the most tested. There's a macOS build for Apple silicon with the same capture-exclusion path. No Linux build yet.

On Groq's small models the first words of the headline land in roughly 150–300 ms and the full short answer in well under a second. Bigger models (70B-class, Claude) trade a second or two of latency for a better answer. It streams either way, so you're reading as it writes.
